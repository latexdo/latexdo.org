What LatexDo does for you.

Every screen below is the real app.

(1)

Errors you can actually read.

When a build fails, LatexDo tells you what went wrong, where, and how to fix it, then takes you to the line. Before you submit, it checks that every figure is found, every citation exists, every label is referenced and every font is embedded.

Common LaTeX errors, explained →
(2)

Citations without the detour.

Search Crossref and OpenAlex from the editor and insert the \cite. The entry goes into your .bib file for you. Then open the knowledge graph to see how your references connect, and find the papers they point to.

(3)

An AI assistant that asks first.

Run a model on your own computer, connect Ollama, or bring your own provider. Every change it proposes is a diff you approve or reject, so nothing in your paper changes behind your back. Pause while writing and it can offer the rest of the sentence.

How the AI in LatexDo works →
(4)

Tables like a spreadsheet.

Type cells or paste them from Excel or Google Sheets, set each column’s alignment, choose grid, booktabs or minimal rules, and get clean LaTeX with a caption and label.

Is LatexDo right for you?

An honest answer, so you don’t have to find out after installing.

A good fit if you…

  • write papers, a thesis or lecture notes in LaTeX;
  • want your files on your own disk and to work offline;
  • are going through peer review and answering reviewers;
  • are new to LaTeX and want help with setup and errors.

Maybe not yet if you…

  • need to work in a browser or on a tablet: LatexDo is a desktop app;
  • don’t want to write LaTeX at all: LatexDo imports Word and Markdown, but you’ll be writing LaTeX afterwards;
  • need software with a long track record: 1.0 was released in October 2026.

Questions, answered.

Something missing? Read the documentation or ask on GitHub.

What is LatexDo?

LatexDo is a free LaTeX editor for macOS, Windows and Linux. It covers the whole paper in one app: writing and compiling, plain-language errors, citations, checks before submission, and answering reviewers.

Do I need LaTeX installed first?

No. If you already have MacTeX, MiKTeX or TeX Live, LatexDo uses it. If you don’t, the setup assistant can install TinyTeX, a compact TeX Live distribution, for you.

Is LatexDo really free?

Yes. LatexDo is free to download and use, and its source code is open under the MIT license. It is built by a French nonprofit association and funded by donations.

Which operating systems are supported?

macOS on Apple Silicon and Intel, 64-bit Windows, and 64-bit Linux as an AppImage.

Where are my files stored?

On your computer. A LatexDo project is an ordinary folder on your disk, so you can keep it in Git, a synced folder or a backup like any other files. There is no account.

Is my paper uploaded anywhere?

No. LatexDo compiles on your computer and does not collect personal analytics or track your documents. Citation search contacts Crossref and OpenAlex with your search terms. If you choose a cloud AI provider, the text you send to the assistant goes to that provider.

Does LatexDo work offline?

Yes. Writing, compiling, checks and history all work offline, and a local AI model works offline once downloaded. Citation search and cloud AI providers need an internet connection.

Can I move my paper from Overleaf or Word?

Yes. You can import an Overleaf project, a Word document, a Markdown file or a searchable PDF and keep working in LaTeX. See the Overleaf comparison.

How mature is LatexDo?

LatexDo 1.0 was released in October 2026 and new builds are published often. The app updates itself and only installs signed releases from latexdo.org. If something doesn’t work, please report it on GitHub.
